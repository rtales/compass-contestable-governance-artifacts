\begin{abstract}
Educational decision systems need inspectable evidence linking decisions to declared policies. We present \compa-\ecl-\mas, an artifact-oriented framework combining empirical constraints, deterministic selection over feasible Pareto candidates, versioned policies, and replay of Top-$K$ decisions with abstention. Its export contract requires both verified evidence and satisfaction of declared empirical restrictions. We evaluate this contract using fixed correctness or student-success scores from ASSISTments, OULAD and a synthetic stress test. Policy sweeps show how capacity, uncertainty thresholds and the treatment of unselected cases affect feasibility and automation coverage. In a retrospective selection/test partition, ASSISTments and OULAD satisfy the sample constraints; synthetic validation has no feasible policy. Seven synthetic fault fixtures show that replay agreement can coexist with incomplete evidence, and verified evidence can coexist with a constraint violation. An exploratory counterbalanced study ($N=16$; eight standardized cases) finds diagnostic and remediation accuracy of 0.5625 with logs/cards and 0.921875 with structured evidence; the two correctness endpoints coincide in every response. The study evaluates the evidence bundle as a whole. The framework provides contestability-enabling infrastructure through reproducible policy analysis, evidence checks and explicit export rejection. Its demonstrated scope is fixed-score decision reconstruction, with institutional appeal processes and prospective educational effects requiring separate evaluation.
\end{abstract}
