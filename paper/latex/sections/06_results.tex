\subsection{Taxonomy and uncertainty}
Table~\ref{tab:policy-replay} fixes scores, $K$ and $\gamma$ while changing abstention semantics. The selected-set positive outcome rate stays fixed in this example, but automation coverage changes because non-selection can be an automated rejection or a deferral. Treating this as a policy correction requires institutional justification; the numbers alone cannot establish which responsibility assignment is appropriate.
\input{tables/policy_replay}

Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty_proxy_validation} reports calibration and entropy/error diagnostics using entropy in bits and average ranks for ties. Here $\rho$ correlates entropy with $|y-s|$, the probabilistic absolute error, rather than the binary classification-error indicator. OULAD has lower entropy/absolute-error monotonicity than ASSISTments; the synthetic predictor is poorly calibrated. Temperature scaling can worsen ECE, so we do not claim calibration robustness from preserved rank correlation. The report includes conditional row-bootstrap intervals and group-specific error counts and Wilson intervals across thresholds. Historical interaction packets lack student cluster IDs, so the ASSISTments intervals do not establish cluster-robust uncertainty or field reliability.

\subsection{Artifact checks and appeal scope}
The schema 2.0 verifier computes integrity and decision-reconstruction checks, with failures tested after packet tampering or removal of policy content. Fitted-model hashes and configuration hashes identify separate objects. The legacy packets contain a configuration digest and a four-point scorecard populated by constant booleans; these fields establish neither fitted-model identity nor independent artifact validation.

The appeal pack (Table~\ref{tab:appeal_scenarios}) is a set of study materials. Medical exceptions, changing income, or removing a feature require additional policy inputs or re-scoring and are outside the fixed-score replay contract. Evidence strings in these materials are not execution logs proving that the released engine implements those operations.

\subsection{Frozen threshold evaluation and failure contracts}
Table~\ref{tab:selection-test} reports the retrospective partition. ASSISTments and OULAD select $\gamma=0.999$ bits on validation. Both chosen policies satisfy the empirical constraints in the test half. The synthetic validation half has zero feasible candidates, so no synthetic test policy is evaluated. This failure is retained; no threshold or capacity is adjusted after seeing it.

Conditional 95\% selected-set positive outcome-rate intervals in test are [0.9742, 0.9901] for ASSISTments and [0.6667, 0.7461] for OULAD. Conservative absolute-gap intervals are [0, 0.0090] and [0, 0.0974], respectively. Thus OULAD's point gap satisfies the 0.05 cap, but its interval does not establish a population gap below that cap. Exact counts, partition hashes, policies, group denominators and intervals are released. These diagnostics do not establish prospective deployment validity.
\input{tables/selection_test}

In the seven fault contracts, the unchanged fixture passes replay, verification and the gate. Four altered fixtures preserve replay agreement (score edit, omitted case, changed model bytes and incompatible units), but fail evidence verification. Missing policy content fails both checks. A fairness-violating fixture passes replay and evidence verification but fails the gate. The omitted-case fixture shows why per-packet hashes require a complete-roster check: hashes of retained packets can remain valid after a case is removed. The result demonstrates the difference between consistency, completeness and authorization under the specified contract, not external authenticity or institutional remedy.

Under the predicates in Section~\ref{sec:export-contract}, the unchanged fixture has $(R,I,A)=(1,1,1)$; the four evidence faults have $(1,0,0)$; missing policy has $(0,0,0)$; and the constraint violation has $(1,1,0)$. The patterns $(1,0,0)$ and $(1,1,0)$ refute sufficiency of replay for full verification and of full verification for export permission, respectively; they do not demonstrate logical independence. The expected/observed checks in Table~\ref{tab:fault-contracts} make the synthetic comparison explicit. Passive logs/cards have no automatic rejection contract here and are not assigned a measured detection score. Blocking an export is the observed software consequence; prevented institutional harm and delivered human review were not evaluated.
