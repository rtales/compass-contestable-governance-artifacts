\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\compa-\ecl-\mas{} connects explicit decision policies, empirical constraints, deterministic feasible-Pareto selection and fixed-score replay through an executable export contract. Its acceptance conditions distinguish reproduction of supplied decisions from verification of complete evidence and from satisfaction of declared export constraints. Seven synthetic fault fixtures demonstrate why replay agreement alone is insufficient for technical export permission.

The educational testbeds show that policy thresholds, capacity and abstention semantics materially affect sample feasibility and automation coverage. The retrospective selection/test analysis preserves the synthetic case's infeasibility instead of selecting a policy on test data. In the exploratory N=16 study, the structured-evidence condition supports higher accuracy on standardized diagnosis and remediation tasks, while the bundled intervention and concordant correctness endpoints limit attribution to individual components.

These findings support an infrastructure contribution: inspectable policy choices, bounded decision reconstruction and explicit rejection when the export contract fails. Institutions must connect that infrastructure to accessible appeals, authorized policy decisions and effective remedies. Prospective evaluation with stakeholders, documented ethical review, independently specified scoring and an instrumented human workflow are needed to establish its value in practice. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} describes the computational artifacts and the access restrictions on participant responses.
