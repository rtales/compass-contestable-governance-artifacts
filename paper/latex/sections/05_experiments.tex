\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Historical data and evaluation scope}
We audit fixed scored test cases from three historical runs: ASSISTments interaction correctness ($n=105107$), OULAD student success ($n=6519$) and a synthetic binary stress test ($n=1000$). ASSISTments uses $y=1$ for a correct response and school-ID parity as a synthetic grouping proxy, not a protected attribute. OULAD uses $y=1$ for Pass/Distinction and binary gender for auditing. Appendix~\ref{app:datasets} gives task and split details. These are outcome-prediction testbeds; descending success scores do not prioritize the highest-risk students for intervention.

The historical runner used a seeded shuffled 80/20 split and seed 42. We do not claim a temporal early-warning horizon, student-cluster independence for interaction rows, or prospective treatment effects. We replay the emitted scores without retraining the predictors. Policy search uses labels from the same retrospective audit sample, so selected operating points are exploratory, not an independently validated final policy.

\subsection{Policy domains}
All datasets use the threshold grid $\{.01,\allowbreak .05,\allowbreak .10,\allowbreak .15,\allowbreak .20,\allowbreak .30,\allowbreak .40,\allowbreak .50,\allowbreak .60,\allowbreak .70,\allowbreak .80,\allowbreak .90,\allowbreak .95,\allowbreak .99,\allowbreak .995,\allowbreak .999\}$ bits. Capacity grids are ASSISTments $\{100,\allowbreak 250,\allowbreak 500,\allowbreak 1000,\allowbreak 2000\}$, OULAD $\{50,100,200,500,1000\}$ and synthetic $\{20,50,100,200,300\}$. Each full grid has 80 candidates. Fixed-budget recovery uses only the 16 thresholds at $K=1000,500,100$, respectively. Full-grid profile comparisons are reported separately. We check gap $\leq.05$ and automation coverage $\geq.50$ and apply the algorithm in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Exploratory contestation study}
\label{sec:human-study}
The study includes 16 volunteers, eight standardized appeal scenarios and 64 case responses per condition. Profiles include engineering/data science, analytics and audit/compliance. Each participant responds to four cases under A (logs/cards) and four under B (structured per-case evidence, policy provenance and replay narratives). G1 receives odd cases in A and even cases in B, with G2 reversed. Each case therefore has eight responses per condition. No participant sees the same case in both conditions. Available study records do not document detailed recruitment procedures, compensation or training.

For each case, participants choose a diagnostic label and remediation action, cite evidence, and rate confidence from 1 to 5. Evidence length is counted in Unicode characters, not words or a quality rubric. Time was self-reported for the questionnaire; we do not estimate per-case staff savings. The case materials include hypothetical exceptions and feature changes beyond the fixed-score replay engine. Consequently this study evaluates availability of structured evidence as a bundle, not execution of every scenario by the software or the isolated causal effect of replay.

\paragraph{Consent and ethics reporting.}
Participation was voluntary, and the forms contain a consent item. The study lead confirmed participant authorization. No formal ethics-committee approval or exemption was obtained. Participant-level responses and free-text evidence are excluded from the public analysis outputs. These records do not establish institutional ethics authorization or permission for public redistribution of individual responses.

\paragraph{Retrospective analysis.}
We calculate participant-level condition averages from the response CSVs and case scoring keys. We use two-sided signed-rank tests by enumerating all sign assignments to nonzero paired midranks; zero differences are excluded and equal absolute differences receive average ranks. No normal approximation or continuity correction is used. Holm adjustment covers diagnosis, remediation, confidence and evidence length. Paired mean differences have percentile 95\% intervals from 10000 participant-bootstrap replicates (seed 42). This analysis was specified after inspecting historical results and is not preregistered. Diagnosis and remediation correctness coincide on all 128 case responses and are not independent replications. Software versions and exact counts accompany the analysis scripts. The eight scoring keys prescribe eight distinct diagnostic categories and eight distinct remediation actions, one pair per scenario. This one-to-one task construction limits construct separation; it does not force respondents to choose both answers consistently. No independently blinded rubric validation is claimed.

\subsection{Retrospective selection/test partition}
\label{sec:selection-test-design}
Before computing split-specific results, we recorded a protocol and partitioned each historical scored sample into validation (floor half) and test (remainder), sorting persistent case IDs by a salted SHA-256 digest. The split uses neither outcomes nor audit groups. At the fixed absolute capacities, we select one threshold from the 16-point grid using validation labels only, feasible-Pareto filtering, the fairness-first profile and zero tolerances. We save all partitions and selected policies with hashes before computing test metrics. If validation has no feasible candidate, we do not substitute a test-selected policy.

The same capacity is applied to each half; Top-$K$ is batch-dependent, so this is not an individual decision invariant to population size. We report all test results without retuning. Wilson intervals describe conditional PosRate and automation coverage. Conservative gap intervals combine two 97.5\% Wilson TPR intervals by Bonferroni. These are row-based diagnostics, not cluster-robust guarantees. The historical data and grid were previously inspected: this is a retrospective policy-transfer check on fixed scores, not a pristine prospective holdout, a new predictor fit or a correction for student-level leakage in the original split.

\subsection{Synthetic fault contracts}
\label{sec:fault-contract-design}
We compare three explicitly limited contracts: passive logs/cards retain information without automated rejection checks; a replay-only checker compares reconstructed decision states with emitted states; the full checker additionally verifies schema, byte hashes, the expected case roster and empirical constraints before authorization. Seven synthetic fixtures cover an unchanged batch, an un-rehashed score edit preserving decisions, an omitted unselected case, changed model bytes, incompatible entropy units, missing policy content and a fairness-violating but replayable policy. This is a functional comparison of specified checks, not an ablation of participant performance or a benchmark against all governance toolkits.
